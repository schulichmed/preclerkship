General approach

Every OB/Gyn history

The general approach

  • Say it first (TOSCE, Teaching Objective Structured Clinical Exam): "Some of the questions I'll ask are personal. I ask them of every patient because they help me understand what's going on. You can skip any question you'd rather not answer."
  • Open the interview: open in a way that creates a professional and trusting setting, so the patient can discuss their concerns openly
    • introduce yourself and your role, confirm the patient's name and pronouns, make sure the door is closed
    • establish rapport and keep it through the whole interview
  • Notice context: identify contextual factors that may shape how you and the patient see things: history of personal violence, sexual orientation, body image, gender, cultural expectations, language and literacy
    • offer an interpreter when needed (the OPR has a box for "Interpreter required")
    • never assume the gender of a partner
  • Watch the patient: recognize non-verbal cues (pauses, looking away, tearfulness) that hint at how the patient feels about the complaint, and name them gently: "This seems hard to talk about."
  • Presenting complaint first: use open-ended questions to let the patient describe the main concern, then ask about its impact on daily life and what they are doing about it now
    • character and site · timing (onset, duration, cyclical, intermittent or continuous) · what makes it better or worse · previous episodes, investigations and treatments · associated symptoms
  • Then tailor: gather the patient's obstetric and gynecologic background, and tailor the rest of the history to what the complaint needs
  • Close: summary, FIFE, a plan in plain words (section 7)

Obstetric history

PoM 2 · OB/Gyn history · 8 areas to ask about

How to open it

What to say

  • Start easy: "How many children have you given birth to?" Then sensitively ask about miscarriages, stillbirths, ectopic pregnancies and terminations.
    • explain why you ask, validate ("That must have been very hard"), and let the patient decide how much to say
    • some patients have not told their current partner about past pregnancies: ask privately
  • Summarize as GTPAL (the OPR's "Pregnancy Summary"): Gravida = all pregnancies, including the current one · Term = delivered at 37 weeks or more · Preterm = delivered 20 to 37 weeks · Abortus = losses before 20 weeks (miscarriage, ectopic, termination) · Living children. The OPR also counts stillbirths and neonatal or child deaths.
    • twins count as one G but two L
  • Then go pregnancy by pregnancy: the OPR's obstetrical history chart has one row per pregnancy: year, place of birth, gestation, labour length, sex, birth weight, type of birth, breastfeeding, the child's current health, and comments.

PoM 2 · OB/Gyn history · 9 areas to ask about

How to open it

What to say

  • Cervical screening: date of the last screening test (Pap/HPV test), the result, and any treatment that followed. If the patient has not been screened, ask gently why: a bad experience or past abuse may be the reason.
  • Past history: pelvic or abdominal surgery · past gynecologic problems (e.g. fibroids, endometriosis) · sexually transmitted infections (STIs)
  • Sexual history: currently active? the 5 Ps: Partners (number and gender), Practices, Protection, Past STIs, Pregnancy prevention; any pain or bleeding

The pregnant patient

PoM 2 · Ontario Perinatal Record · 8 areas to ask about

How to open it

What to say

  • Use the Ontario Perinatal Record as your guide for a comprehensive obstetric history. Work through it in its order; it doubles as your checklist.
  • Identify and date the pregnancy: LMP, cycle length and regularity, contraceptive type and when last used, planned pregnancy?, assisted conception? (IUI, intrauterine insemination, or embryo transfer dates), estimated date of birth (EDB) by LMP, by first- or second-trimester ultrasound (T1US, T2US), and the final EDB
    • Naegele's rule: EDB = LMP + 7 days − 3 months + 1 year
  • Tests so far: "Have you had an ultrasound or any blood tests in this pregnancy?" The OPR records the dating ultrasound, whether prenatal genetic screening was offered (e.g. a nuchal translucency (NT) ultrasound at 11 to 13+6 weeks, non-invasive prenatal testing (NIPT)) and the anatomy scan at 18 to 22 weeks.
  • How do you feel about this pregnancy?: excited, anxious or unplanned

Wrapping up

PoM 2 · OB/Gyn history

How to open it

What to say

  • Summarize: read back what you heard and ask for corrections; it often prompts the patient to add something important.
  • FIFE: ask about the patient's Feelings, Ideas, Function and Expectations. It guides the discussion of next steps.
  • Differential: formulate and prioritize a differential diagnosis and a preliminary management plan from what you heard
    • ⚠ post-menopausal bleeding: endometrial cancer until proven otherwise
    • ⚠ bleeding in pregnancy: first trimester, think miscarriage, ectopic pregnancy, molar pregnancy or an implantation bleed; third trimester, think placenta previa (painless) or placental abruption (painful)
  • Plan in plain words: answer questions in lay terms and give a clear plan of care, checking the patient understands and agrees
  • Present it: summarize the encounter to present to peers and faculty: identifying data and GTPAL, the presenting complaint, pertinent positives and negatives, your differential and plan.
