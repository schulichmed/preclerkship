General approach

Every ultrasound

The general approach

  • Say it first (TOSCE): "Hello, I'm ___, a medical student. Can I check your name and date of birth? I'd like to do a quick bedside ultrasound of your [heart / abdomen]. I'll put some cool gel on your skin and press gently with the probe. It doesn't hurt and uses no radiation. Is that alright?" Then wash your hands, lie the patient flat on their back (supine), and uncover only the area you are scanning.
  • Choose the probe: the curved (curvilinear) probe, or the small flat-faced phased array probe, for the heart and abdomen: they use lower-frequency sound that reaches deeper
  • Probe marker: the ridge or dot on one side of the probe; it matches a dot on the screen
    • for a lengthwise view (sagittal front-to-back slice, or coronal side-on slice) the marker points to the patient's head
    • for a cross-section (transverse / axial) it points to the patient's right
  • Reading the screen: the top of the screen is the skin under the probe (near field); the bottom is deepest (far field)
  • Words for brightness (echogenicity)
    • anechoic = black: fluid (blood, bile, urine)
    • hypoechoic = darker grey than the tissue around it
    • isoechoic = the same grey as the tissue around it (liver, spleen, kidney cortex)
    • hyperechoic = bright white: bone, the diaphragm, fat around the kidney
  • Acoustic window: sound travels well through solid organs and fluid, badly through air and bone. So you look through the liver (to the heart, the kidney), or through a full bladder (to the pelvis)

Heart: the subxiphoid view

PoM 1 · POCUS session · 1 views

Before you scan

The view

  • The probe sits just below the breastbone (under the xiphoid process) and looks up through the liver at the heart; the liver is the window, lying directly under the probe
  • Name the structures: right ventricle, left ventricle, right atrium, left atrium, interventricular septum (the wall between the ventricles), pericardium (the sac around the heart)
  • Show the cardiac cycle: systole (the ventricles squeeze and get smaller) and diastole (they relax and fill)
Tap to reveal

Is the heart moving, and is there fluid around it?

Subxiphoid (heart)

All four chambers, the septum and the pericardium, beating

Steps
  1. Patient supine; curved or phased array probe
  2. Start with the probe flat on the umbilicus and slide it up to the xiphoid
  3. Look up through the liver at the heart
  4. Can't see the heart? Inspiratory hold (a deep breath held pushes the diaphragm down and brings the heart closer) · bend the knees (relaxes the abdominal muscles)
A good view

Good view = liver nearest the probe, then the heart, with right and left ventricles, right and left atria and the septum visible, the pericardium around them, moving through systole and diastole

Upper abdomen

PoM 1 · POCUS session · 3 views

Before you scan

The view

  • Right upper quadrant (RUQ): liver, right kidney, diaphragm
    • liver = isoechoic (even grey)
    • kidney = a "double density": grey outer cortex, bright white centre (the renal sinus: collecting system with the calyces and renal pelvis, fat and the main blood vessels)
    • the slit between liver and kidney is the hepatorenal space (Morison's pouch)
    • diaphragm = a bright white curved line above the liver
    • the spine shows deep in the far field as a bright "railroad track"
  • Gallbladder: the gallbladder and the portal triad (portal vein, hepatic artery, common bile duct)
    • gallbladder = anechoic (black, full of bile)
    • the exclamation-point sign: the gallbladder is the line, the portal triad is the dot (shaped like a Mickey Mouse head), and the main lobar fissure (a thin bright line in the liver) joins them
  • Left upper quadrant (LUQ): spleen, left kidney, diaphragm
    • spleen = isoechoic · left kidney = double density · diaphragm = bright white · the lung sits above the diaphragm

Liver, kidney and the space between them

Right upper quadrant

Liver, right kidney, diaphragm

Steps
  1. Patient supine; curved probe
  2. Probe on the right side, mid-axillary line, level with the xiphoid, in a coronal (side-on) plane
  3. Marker to the patient's head
  4. Slide toward the feet or the head until the kidney appears
A good view

Good view = liver above, kidney below, Morison's pouch between them, the diaphragm as a bright line, the spine deep

Gallbladder

Gallbladder and portal triad

Steps
  1. Probe under the right costal margin (edge of the ribs), midclavicular line
  2. Sagittal plane, marker to the patient's head
  3. Fan slowly until a black, bag-shaped structure appears
A good view

Good view = the exclamation point: black gallbladder (line), main lobar fissure, portal triad (dot)

Pelvis

PoM 1 · POCUS session · 2 views

Before you scan

The view

  • Name the structures: bladder, uterus or prostate, rectum
  • The bladder must be full: urine is the acoustic window into the pelvis
  • Male: bladder (anechoic) → prostate below it → rectum below that; note the rectovesical space (between bladder and rectum)
  • Female: bladder (anechoic) → uterus (isoechoic) in the far field, with a bright line in its centre (the endometrium, the uterine lining) → rectum below; note the vesicouterine space (between bladder and uterus) and the rectouterine pouch (pouch of Douglas) behind the uterus
    • in the sagittal view you also see the vagina as a darker (hypoechoic) band behind the bladder

Bladder and what lies behind it

Pelvis, transverse

Bladder, uterus or prostate, rectum

Steps
  1. Patient supine with a full bladder; curved probe
  2. Probe across the lower abdomen (transverse), marker to the patient's right
  3. Slide down to the pubic symphysis (pubic bone), then tilt the probe down into the pelvis
A good view

Good view = black bladder at the top, uterus or prostate behind it, rectum deepest

Vessels

T2C · POCUS

Before you scan

The view

  • No source covers these views in detail: only the objectives are given here; learn the probe positions in session
  • Abdominal aorta view: identify the abdominal aorta and the inferior vena cava (IVC)
  • Carotid sheath view (the bundle in the side of the neck): identify the carotid artery and the internal jugular vein (IJV)
    • Valsalva (bearing down against a closed throat): show its effect on the internal jugular vein
    • Compression with the probe: show its effect on the internal jugular vein and the carotid artery
