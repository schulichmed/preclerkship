General approach

Every interview

The general approach

  • Say it first (TOSCE, Teaching Objective Structured Clinical Exam): "Hello, my name is [name], I'm a medical student working with Dr. [X]. I'd like to get to know you and hear about what's been going on. Is it okay if I ask you some questions today?"
  • Explain the notes: "I'll be taking a few notes as we talk so I can share them with the healthcare team and make sure your care best reflects your experience."
    • tell the patient what they say is confidential (shared only with the care team)
    • keep note taking seamless: it must not interfere with the conversation or rapport
  • Settle the patient: comfort first: lights, noise, seating
  • Confirm identity (patient identification): name and age, then the patient's gender as they describe it
  • Build rapport without words: eye contact, facial expressions, posture, positioning and movement, vocal expression (rate, volume and tone)
    • session note: stay smiling, stay conversational and empathetic
  • Communication skills: encourage the patient to tell their own story; listen without interrupting; facilitate responses with words and nods; avoid ambiguity and clarify; notice verbal and non-verbal cues (body language, speech, facial expression, affect); summarize periodically so the patient can confirm; use plain (lay) language that invites questions
    • ask why and how, then give the patient time to answer
    • follow up anything out of the ordinary
  • Professional behaviour: respect, reliability, honesty, courtesy, acceptance of difference, clarity, respecting patient–physician boundaries; non-judgmental; respect confidentiality, its limits, and autonomy
  • Rapport: accept the patient's views and feelings, use empathy, provide a supportive environment, offer partnership, deal with topics in a sensitive manner

Chief complaint

FoM · Introduction to Interviewing

How to open it

What to say

  • Open: "What brings you in today? What's the main thing you'd like us to focus on together?"
  • Get the whole agenda early: "Is there anything else on your mind that you'd like to make sure we cover?"
  • Address the patient-driven HPI first: let them lead before you take over with your questions
  • Respect the patient's agenda without losing sight of the physician's agenda: if the patient brings several concerns, agree together which to focus on today
  • With a patient who keeps drifting: "I'd like to use this time to focus on what brought you in today. Can you tell me more about…?"

Closing

FoM · Introduction to Interviewing

How to open it

What to say

  • Summarize all key concerns back to the patient, so they can correct you and you catch anything missed; then follow up and probe more (there is time: the interview is about 25 minutes, no need to rush the end)
  • Check emotions: "We have spent some time talking about your physical health. Do you have any concerns about the way you feel emotionally?"
  • Open the door: "That's everything I wanted to cover today. Before we move to the physical exam, is there anything else that feels important for me to know or that you'd like to bring up?"
  • Thank them: "Thank you so much for helping me understand what you've been experiencing. It really helps me see what's been going on and think about how we can plan your care in a way that best supports you."
  • Lay the groundwork for finding common ground: understand the patient's agenda and clarify yours · explore possible causes and the illness experience in context · explain the problem and proposed management clearly · give the patient a chance to ask questions · enable mutual discussion · check that you agree
    • common ground on three things: the nature of the problems and priorities, the goals of treatment, and the roles of the doctor and the patient
  • Develop a therapeutic relationship that acknowledges the patient's concerns

Advanced interviewing

PoM 1 · Advanced Interviewing · 23 areas to ask about

How to open it

What to say

  • Same spine as every interview, with complex medical, social and/or ethical issues. No course notes cover this block yet, so the approaches below are standard frameworks: check them against your session.
  • One approach for every hard situation: Prepare (private space, know the facts) → Ask permission → Listen first → Respond to the emotion (name it, understand, respect, support, explore) → Explain plainly → Check understanding → Agree the next step
    • Talk to the patient directly, always: face them, eye contact, say "you"; with a translator or interpreter, speak to the patient and not to the translator
    • use short sentences, one question at a time, and hand gestures and body language to carry your meaning
    • the patient is the expert on their own identity, culture and story: ask, don't assume, and notice your own biases
    • say the limits of confidentiality before the patient discloses; stuck or unsure: involve your supervisor and document
